02

A direct relationship

Speak to the party considering the purchase.

We evaluate each business for ourselves. The first conversation is not a sales pitch or the beginning of a marketed process; it is a practical exchange about the company, your priorities, and possible fit.

If mutual interest develops, the information becomes more detailed. Until then, the conversation stays appropriately focused.

  1. 01

    Direct engagement

    Talk with the prospective acquirer rather than an intermediary searching for one.

  2. 02

    Disciplined discretion

    Share information in stages as both parties decide whether to continue.

  3. 03

    Context-led terms

    Consider timing, structure, and the owner's role in the context of the business.

  4. 04

    Operating respect

    Understand the people and practices behind performance before proposing change.

After the transactionPeople / Customers / Continuity / Context

A company is more than the numbers that describe it.

Its strength also lives in the judgment of its people, the trust of customers, and the routines that turn promises into results.

Our starting point is to learn those foundations before deciding what should continue and where thoughtful improvement may be useful.

05 Common questions

Clarity before commitment.

01Do I need to be actively selling my business?

No. An initial conversation can begin while you are considering timing and alternatives.

02Is the first conversation confidential?

We intend to handle introductions discreetly. Sensitive information should follow appropriate protections and a secure process.

03What role can an owner have after a transaction?

That depends on the owner's objectives and the needs of the business. Different transition paths may be considered.

04Are the financial ranges absolute?

No. They are current acquisition guidelines, and each company is considered on its own facts.

05Is Hyperboards a broker or adviser?

No. Hyperboards evaluates opportunities as the prospective buyer and does not market businesses to third-party acquirers.
