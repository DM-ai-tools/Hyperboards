## Design Context

### Users

Established U.S. business owners considering a confidential sale, plus a secondary audience of aligned acquisition-capital partners. Owners need to understand quickly that Hyperboards is the direct prospective buyer—not a broker, investment bank or adviser—and decide whether a private conversation is worthwhile.

### Brand Personality

Discreet, assured and materially crafted. The experience should feel like a well-made navy leather document case: serious enough for an acquisition conversation, warm enough for an owner-led transition, and distinctive without becoming theatrical.

### Aesthetic Direction

Editorial family-office restraint with a navy-first palette, warm paper surfaces, controlled brass hardware and fine saddle stitching. Leather imagery is used only as a low-contrast relief texture over the original navy—not as a photographic color field. Motion is sparse, quiet and purposeful. Avoid glossy finance clichés, generic card grids, oversized decoration and effects that weaken readability.

### Design Principles

1. Preserve the original navy identity; texture supports color rather than replacing it.
2. Communicate direct-buyer credibility before visual novelty.
3. Use material hierarchy consistently: navy leather, warm paper, then restrained brass.
4. Keep seams and hardware physically plausible through scale, lighting and depth.
5. Use one signature chart motion plus subtle interaction feedback, always with reduced-motion support.
