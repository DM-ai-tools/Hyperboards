## Design Context

### Users

Established U.S. business owners considering a confidential sale, plus a secondary audience of aligned acquisition-capital partners. Owners need to understand quickly that Hyperboards is the direct prospective buyer—not a broker, investment bank or adviser—and decide whether a private conversation is worthwhile.

### Brand Personality

Discreet, assured and materially composed. The experience should feel serious enough for an acquisition conversation, warm enough for an owner-led transition, and distinctive without becoming theatrical.

### Aesthetic Direction

Editorial family-office restraint expressed through two approved directions: a lighter navy leather-and-paper system without stitching or visible hardware, and an Evergreen system built from limestone, layered forest tones and restrained copper detail. Leather remains a low-contrast relief texture rather than a photographic color field. Motion is sparse, quiet and purposeful. Avoid glossy finance clichés, generic card grids, oversized decoration and effects that weaken readability.

### Design Principles

1. Preserve the softened navy identity; texture supports color rather than replacing it.
2. Communicate direct-buyer credibility before visual novelty.
3. Use material hierarchy consistently: lighter navy leather, warm paper and restrained brass—without decorative seams or screws.
4. Keep Evergreen minimal; richer forest shades, typography and surface depth provide refinement without changing its structure.
5. Use one signature motion moment plus subtle interaction feedback, always with reduced-motion support.
